What comes from the papyri

In the capitals, A is shaped like alpha. E and C are the rounded forms scribes used. M is the rounded mu. P is shaped like rho and drops below the line. W is shaped like omega. Stems start with a small flag and end in a hook, and the ink pools where the pen lands. F, G, J, L, Q, R, S, U and V are not in the Greek alphabet, so they are drawn with the same pen.

O is written small, as the scribes wrote it. When a capital O starts a word in lowercase, as in “Oh”, it grows to full height so it still reads as a capital.

The lowercase

These manuscripts have capitals only, so the small letters are new. They are written with the same pen, held a little finer. Where a Greek letter looks like ours it lends its shape: a from alpha, e from the rounded epsilon and w from omega. The d leans back, as it does in early Latin book hands.

Every letter is written three slightly different ways, and the font cycles through them as you type.
